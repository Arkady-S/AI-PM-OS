Uncovering psychological and tribal jobs beneath functional requests

## CORE CONCEPT

Users describe functional jobs. They hire products for emotional and social ones.

When Anthropic asked 81,000 users what they wanted from AI, 19% said "professional excellence." Probing revealed the real job: get time back for family, hobbies, rest. The functional ask (faster reports) masked the psychological job (feel less consumed by work).

Every functional job has emotional and social layers underneath. Products that address only the functional layer compete on features. Products that reach the emotional and social layers become hard to leave.

## THREE JOB LAYERS

| Layer | Question It Answers | Example (Project Mgmt) |
|---|---|---|
| Functional | What task am I trying to complete? | Track tasks and deadlines across my team |
| Emotional | How do I want to feel? | Feel in control, not anxious about what I'm missing |
| Social / Tribal | How do I want to be perceived? | Be seen as the organized, reliable leader |

Most PMs stop at the functional layer. Competitors match functional jobs within 6 months. Emotional and social jobs are where durable differentiation lives, because they're tied to identity, not features.

## THE DEPTH LADDER

A structured sequence for moving from surface to depth. Each question targets a deeper layer. Pause after each, let silence do the work.

### 1. Functional: "Walk me through what happened"

Establish the concrete context. What were they doing, what tools, what sequence. Don't interpret yet.

### 2. Stakes: "What happens if this goes wrong"

Surfaces the weight of the task. Reveals whether the job is high- or low-stakes, and for whom.

### 3. Emotional: "How did that make you feel"

Direct emotional probe. If they deflect, try: "Was that stressful?" or "Were you worried about that?" Name the emotion for them.

### 4. Identity: "What kind of [role] do you want to be"

Connects the task to self-concept. "What kind of manager do you want to be?" reveals the aspirational identity the product serves.

### 5. Tribal: "What would your team/peers think"

Surfaces social perception anxiety. How they want to be seen by colleagues, leadership, or their professional community.

## EMOTIONAL JOB PATTERNS

Common psychological jobs users won't volunteer. Listen for the language in the right column during interviews:

| Emotional Job | What They Say Instead |
|---|---|
| Reduce anxiety | "I just need to know nothing is falling through the cracks" |
| Feel competent | "I don't want to look like I don't know what I'm doing" |
| Regain control | "Everything moves so fast, I can't keep up" |
| Avoid blame | "If something goes wrong, I need to show I did my part" |
| Feel progress | "Some weeks I have no idea if I accomplished anything" |
| Reduce cognitive load | "I'm just tired of thinking about this all the time" |

## TRIBAL / SOCIAL JOB PATTERNS

Social jobs are about how people want to be perceived. They're outward-facing and tied to group identity, status, and belonging:

| Social Job | Probing Question |
|---|---|
| Signal competence to leadership | "What does your manager see when this works well?" |
| Belong to the "modern" cohort | "How does your team talk about people who use/don't use this?" |
| Protect professional reputation | "What would happen to your credibility if this failed?" |
| Lead by example for team | "What does adopting this say about you to your reports?" |
| Avoid being the bottleneck | "Has anyone ever had to wait on you for this? How did that feel?" |

## 5 SIGNALS YOU'VE HIT A DEEP JOB

### 1. Energy Shift

They lean in, speak faster, or get quieter. Emotional resonance changes body language.

### 2. Unprompted Storytelling

They volunteer a specific anecdote without you asking. The story is the data.

### 3. "I Know This Sounds Stupid, But..."

Vulnerability preambles signal the real job. Whatever follows the qualifier is gold.

### 4. They Name a Specific Person

"My VP" or "my skip-level" appearing means the social/tribal dimension is active.

### 5. Contradiction with Stated Priority

They say speed matters most but spend 20 minutes describing accuracy anxiety. The contradiction reveals the emotional job.

## COMMON FAILURE MODES

| Failure | Symptom | Prevention |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Staying functional | Features match competitors in 6 months | Use Depth Ladder; push past functional layer |
| Missing energy shifts | Surface-level insights only | Watch for body language, pace changes, unprompted storytelling |
| Ignoring contradictions | Wrong priority in product spec | When stated priority contradicts time spent, probe the contradiction |
| Premature interpretation | Projecting hypothesis onto their words | Let them finish; don't interpret during the interview |
| No tribal probing | Product ignores social/status needs | Ask "What would your team/peers think?" |
