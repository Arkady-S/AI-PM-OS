Discovering problems worth solving through honest conversation

**March 2026**

## CORE CONCEPT

> Your job is to find truth, not collect validation. Bad feedback is worse than no feedback because it sends you down the wrong path with false confidence.
> (Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test)

Stated needs differ from real needs. Anthropic's 81,000-person study found users who said "productivity" actually wanted time freedom: leaving work on time, cooking with family, reading more books. Always ask what the underlying hope is behind the stated goal.

## THE MOM TEST: 3 RULES

### 1. Talk about their life, not your idea.

If you avoid mentioning your idea, you automatically start asking better questions. Never ask if your idea is good. Learn about their problems, context, and constraints instead.

### 2. Ask about specifics in the past, not the future.

People are overly optimistic about hypothetical futures. "Would you use X?" always gets a yes. "What did you do last time this happened?" reveals truth.

### 3. Talk less and listen more.

The more you talk, the more you bias. Your goal is to learn, not to pitch. When you catch yourself explaining, stop and ask a question instead.

## QUESTION QUALITY

| Bad Question (Fails Mom Test) | Good Question (Passes) |
|------|--------|
| "Do you think this is a good idea?" | "How do you deal with this today?" |
| "Would you buy a product that did X?" | "What have you tried? What happened?" |
| "How much would you pay for X?" | "What does this problem cost you now?" |
| "Do you like this feature?" | "Walk me through the last time this came up." |
| "Would you use this every day?" | "How often did this happen last month?" |

## THREE TYPES OF BAD DATA

| Type | What It Sounds Like | What to Do |
|------|-----|--------|
| Compliments | "That's a great idea!" "I'd definitely use that." | Deflect. Ask what they do today instead. |
| Hypothetical fluff | "I would probably..." "I think I might..." | Anchor to the past. "When did you last..." |
| Feature requests | "It would be cool if it could also..." | Dig into motivation. "Why would that matter?" |

## AI-ERA RESEARCH UPGRADES

Findings from Anthropic's 81K qualitative study and AI lab product practices that extend classical interview methods:

> Probe beneath surface answers. Anthropic's AI interviewer asked follow-up questions to get past initial responses. When users said "productivity," probing revealed the real goal was time with family.

> Reduced social cost gets honesty. Users disclosed more to an AI interviewer than in typical surveys. In human interviews, create this same safety through warmth and nonjudgment.

> Frequency is a bad proxy for importance. Anthropic found emotional support was 2.9% of traffic but a top category in both benefits and fears. Don't let usage volume dictate priority.

> Hopes and fears coexist, not separate. Every benefit maps to a fear. Ask about both. The same capability that helps a user is often the one they worry about most.

> Prototype before you spec. Anthropic optimizes for speed to learning: build fast, ship internally, learn from real usage. The prototype becomes the spec.

## INTERVIEW STRUCTURE

| Phase | Time | What to Do |
|-------|------|-----------|
| Frame | 2 min | State purpose. "Learning about how you handle X." No pitch. |
| Context | 5 min | "Walk me through the last time..." Map their world. |
| Pain | 5 min | "Where do you get stuck?" "What did you try?" Listen for emotion. |
| Depth | 5 min | "Why does that matter?" "What happens if you don't solve it?" |
| Close | 3 min | Seek commitment: intro, follow-up, pilot. Not "thanks, great chat." |

## COMMITMENT LADDER

Compliments are free. Commitment costs something. Escalate asks to test whether pain is real:

| Level | Signal | Strength |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Time | Agrees to a follow-up conversation | Weak |
| Reputation | Introduces you to colleagues or decision-makers | Medium |
| Effort | Participates in a pilot or trial with real work | Strong |
| Money | Pre-pays, signs LOI, or allocates budget | Strongest |

## COMMON FAILURE MODES

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Pitching, not learning | You talked more than they did | Ban your idea for the first 15 minutes |
| Collecting compliments | Everyone says "great idea" | Ask what they're doing about it today |
| Too broad a segment | Contradictory signals across users | Narrow until patterns converge |
| Hypothetical answers | "I would definitely..." | Redirect: "Tell me about last time." |
| Skipping commitment | Good vibes but no next step | Always ask for a concrete next action |
| Solo synthesis | One person interprets all notes | Share exact quotes; team reviews together |
